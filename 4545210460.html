Description

Antique copper jelly mold with a deep fluted melon crown, a stepped faceted base, a tin-lined interior, and its original riveted copper hanging loop still in place. 

Measures 6" across the bottom and 3 1/4"  tall.

This one was made the old way, and the photographs show it. The body is raised from heavy-gauge sheet copper rather than stamped from thin sheet — the flutes hold their shape crisply and the panel edges stay sharp. The working seam is still readable where the fluted crown meets the base skirt. The hanging tab is riveted through the rolled rim, a mechanical fixing rather than a modern solder joint, and its rivet head is visible from inside. The interior is tinned, the traditional food-safe lining put on copper cookware long before stainless steel existed. Together those are the marks of a genuine working kitchen mold rather than a decorative reproduction.

The form is the classic jelly and pudding shape: a domed crown of deep flutes radiating from a round central button, set on a faceted skirt of flat panels with a rolled foot rim. Turned out, it gives a ribbed melon with a crisp collar at the base. The dome is solid and there is no centre tube, so this is a jelly, pudding and aspic mold rather than a bundt or Kugelhopf pan.

There is no maker's mark, no stamped number and no size numeral anywhere on the piece — not on the exterior, the base rim, or the interior where a stamp would normally sit. That is common on unmarked English and continental kitchen copper of this type, and it is worth saying plainly: everything above is read from the construction, not from a signature.
