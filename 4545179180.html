Description

A vintage wooden fish scaling board with a spring-loaded steel tail clamp — a working lake-cabin tool that has aged into a genuinely handsome object. The hardwood blade carries a panel of deep parallel V-grooves down its center, and the steel ferrule capping the handle is stamped TAIWAN.

Boards like this were the standard fish-cleaning rig on docks and back porches from the 1960s through the 1980s, sold across sporting goods counters and bait shops under names like Normark and Sharp and, as here, with no maker's name at all. The clamp bites the tail, and the grooved panel holds a slippery fish still while the scaler works down its flank. Every line of the shape belongs to that job — the long taper, the wide flat blade, and the metal cap that keeps the handle from splitting under the spring's pull.

The wood is the best thing about this particular example. It has an even honey color over tight straight grain, the corners are still softly rounded rather than chewed, and the V-grooves are crisp and unworn — a real departure from the grey, weather-stripped boards these usually turn up as. The reverse face is plain and smooth, with light water shadowing and no grooves at all.

DATING: The word TAIWAN is stamped on both sides of the pressed steel ferrule. That country-of-origin marking convention, together with the pressed sheet-steel hardware and the machine-cut groove panel, places the board in roughly the 1960s to 1980s. There is no maker's mark anywhere on the wood or the clamp, which is normal for these — the majority were sold unbranded or under a retailer's name rather than a manufacturer's.
