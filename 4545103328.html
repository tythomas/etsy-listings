Description

An eight-piece antique silverplate cocktail service: a Crescent Silver cocktail shaker with six matching stemmed champagne coupes, presented on a 12 1/4 inch Webster-Wilcox "Brandon Hall" gallery tray. The shaker stands approximately 11 inches tall.

This is the kind of set that came out when people still dressed for drinks. The shaker and the six coupes are both Crescent Silver Mfg. Co. of Port Jervis, New York — a firm founded in 1922 that spent its whole life making silver-plated hollowware, and whose Art Deco cocktail pitchers and cups are the pieces collectors still look for. The tray joined them later. It is a Webster-Wilcox "Brandon Hall" pattern gallery tray by the International Silver Company, and it earns its place: the pierced gallery holds all six stems from sliding, and the engraved floral field is the right scale under them.

The shaker is a cocktail pitcher form rather than a three-piece bar shaker. The body tapers from a stepped foot to a shouldered top, with a scrolled wing handle, a stepped domed lid, and a short spout closed by a knurled screw cap so the whole thing can be shaken and then poured. A band of shell-and-scroll repousse work wraps the shoulder — the one piece of ornament on an otherwise plain body, and the detail that dates the design to the Deco years.

The six coupes match it in style and finish: a wide flared bowl on a slim fluted stem over a broad stepped foot, with a fine beaded line around the underside of each bowl.

Each goblet foot is stamped CRESCENT SILVER MFG around a crescent-moon device — the maker's name in full, no guesswork. The shaker's base carries Crescent's three-shield pseudo-hallmark (a rampant beast, a crescent-and-C, and a spread-wing bird) over the model number 7154, the same crescent device without the spelled-out name. The tray's reverse is engraved Webster-Wilcox / INTERNATIONAL SILVER CO / Brandon Hall / 7571G, and 7571G is the documented 12 1/4 inch round in that line. Photographs of all three marks are included.

Dating: Crescent operated from 1922 until 1977, so the mark alone does not fix a year; the form places the shaker and coupes in the 1930s-40s. Webster-Wilcox is a later International Silver brand, so the tray is a mid-century addition to an older pair of pieces. This is an assembled service, not a factory-boxed set, and it is described that way honestly.
