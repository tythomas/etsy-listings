Description

Antique brown stoneware crock with a rich Albany-type slip glaze, thick rolled rim and wonderful primitive character. This unmarked utilitarian preserve/storage jar dates approximately 1880-1920.

The glaze ranges naturally from reddish and chocolate brown to very dark brown/near black, with amber-toned areas from the original firing. Wheel/throwing marks and handmade surface irregularities add to the character of this early stoneware piece.

MEASUREMENTS
• 7" tall
• 5 3/4" outside diameter
• 4 5/8" inside rim opening
• 6 3/4" interior depth
• 5 3/4" base diameter

CONDITION
Good antique condition with age-appropriate wear. No cracks, hairlines, repairs or restoration noted. The stoneware rim is solid with scattered small nicks/chips and losses to the brown glaze, including several along the thick rolled rim and near the lower edge, as shown in the photos. Additional small glaze skips, pits and surface irregularities are present.

The variations from reddish and chocolate brown to very dark brown/black are natural variations in the fired glaze rather than later discoloration. Interior shows normal age/use wear with no significant staining, residue, mineral deposits or odors noted.

Unmarked; maker is unknown.

The crock has only been wiped with a damp cloth and has not been washed or soaked.

A handsome piece of early American utilitarian stoneware for primitive kitchen, farmhouse or pantry decor, or for displaying utensils or dried florals.
