Description

Antique Trip Around the World patchwork quilt in soft Depression-era pastels, hand quilted throughout, measuring 96 x 90 inches. Concentric diamonds of pink, lavender, blue, sage and butter yellow ripple out from a single green center square to the edge of the quilt

The small squares are set on point in concentric bands, so each ring of color frames the one inside it and the whole reads as a bullseye from across a room. Printed calicoes alternate against plain solids in every band. Because the squares sit on point, the outer edge finishes in a sawtooth zigzag rather than a straight binding, with top and backing turned in against each other and stitched closed along every point - fussy, patient work.

The prints are small-scale dress cottons of the type used through the 1930s and into the 1940s: white-outline florals on colored grounds, tiny sprigged calicoes, dotted grounds, little tulips and daisies, and navy-on-white shirting prints. Several look like the printed feed sack cloth of those years, though I have not verified any individual square and do not claim it as fact. The solids are period cottons, and their condition supports the dating: they have worn thinnest, which is what happens to early piece-dyed cottons after eighty-odd years. The quilting is plain running stitch worked diagonally through the squares, roughly six to eight stitches to the inch - utility quilting, done by someone who meant the quilt to be used.
