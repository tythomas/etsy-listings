Description

Set of four Anchor Hocking "Berwick" coupes — the pattern collectors call "Boopie" — with a gold band at the rim and the pattern's signature ring of clear glass beads around the foot. 3 1/2" tall, 3 5/8" across the rim, holds 6 oz.

Anchor Hocking pressed the Berwick line in Lancaster, Ohio from the late 1940s into the 1960s. Collectors nicknamed it "Boopie," though the factory's own catalogs list it as Berwick. These are the gold-rimmed version, which turns up far less often than the plain clear.

The bowl is a plain conical coupe with no cutting or etching; the stem is a short pressed knop over a rayed, paneled cone. The beads at each foot are solid glass formed in the mold, not applied — the molded rays and the unbroken ring of beads show clearly in the close-ups.

PATTERN: Berwick, known to collectors as "Boopie"

MARK: None. Anchor Hocking did not mark Berwick stemware, so the missing backstamp is expected — the beaded foot and rayed, paneled stem are the identifying features, both shown here.

Care: Hand wash only — the gold band is applied over the glass and will not survive a dishwasher.

Condition: Good vintage condition with honest wear
• The gold rims show wear. On at least one glass the band has thinned and darkened along a section of the rim (visible in the close-ups) — loss of the gilding, not soil, and it will not polish out.
• Fine dark specks and light patchy dulling along the gold band on more than one glass.
• The bowls are clear and bright, with no cloudiness or dishwasher haze.
