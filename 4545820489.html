Description

An oval serving platter in Edwin Knowles' Spring Bouquet pattern, 13.5 inches long, with a deeply fluted ivory rim and a single floral transfer in the well. One platter, dated to October 1943.

Edwin M. Knowles potted this at Newell, West Virginia, and stamped it with a date code, so it can be placed to the month, not the decade. The mark reads EDWIN M. KNOWLES CHINA CO. / MADE IN U.S.A. over the figure 43-10 — the tenth month of 1943. It is wartime dinnerware on the semi-vitreous body Knowles used for everyday services, and the absence of gold trim — a period economy — is what makes it easy to live with now.

The pattern is Spring Bouquet, catalogued by Replacements as KNOSPB — "Fluted Rim, Pink/Purple Floral Bouquet" — circa 1941. The bouquet is a polychrome transfer, not hand painting: every petal carries the same even printed edge. It sits off-center in the well, so a carved roast still leaves the flowers showing. Crimson and pink peonies open around a pale lavender anemone with a gold-and-maroon center, with blue forget-me-nots and a trailing spray of larkspur running toward the rim. The rim is the reason to want this shape: deep vertical flutes cut the whole way around a wide flat border and cast their own shadow, which is what gives a plain white platter its architecture.
