Description

A set of eight antique Priscilla dinner plates, made by the Homer Laughlin China Company for Household Institute and date-marked July 1947. Ivory china with a scalloped, embossed border, a gold rim band, and a rose-and-tulip bouquet at the center. 10 1/4" across.

Household Institute was never a pottery. It was a 1941 agreement between Homer Laughlin and the Club Aluminum Company, which moved dinnerware through grocery-store continuity programs — a plate a week with the shopping, a service built one trip at a time. Priscilla ran from roughly 1941 until it was discontinued around 1950.

The evidence for the date is on the backs, and it is photographed here. Three marks: a gold ring reading HOUSEHOLD INSTITUTE around "Priscilla" in script; the green underglaze Homer Laughlin mark with MADE IN U.S.A.; and the code G 47 N 8 — G is the seventh letter and stands for July, 47 for 1947, N for the Newell, West Virginia plant, 8 for the plant number. A red painted bar appears on some backs; that is a factory selector's mark, not damage.

The decoration is transfer-printed and hand-tinted, not hand-painted. The center bouquet — a full pink cabbage rose, a striped magenta-and-yellow tulip, purple asters and small blue florals — repeats identically from plate to plate, which is what a transfer does and a brush does not. The scattered rim sprigs were applied by hand, so their spacing shifts a little between plates; that variation is original to the ware, not a fault. Underneath it all, the scalloped edge is pressed with a scroll-and-shell relief, and the gold band follows every curve of it.

Hand wash only. Not for dishwasher and microwave usage. As with any American decal-and-gold ware of this age, decorative and gentle use is recommended over daily service.
