Description

Antique Buckeye Pottery yellowware pitcher with a warm mustard-yellow glaze and paired cobalt-blue bands around the upper and lower body.  * NOTE- there is a crack in the side of this piece - see pictures

Marked Buckeye on the underside, this early American utility pitcher was produced by the Buckeye Pottery Company of Macomb, Illinois. Buckeye was established in 1882 and operated until its purchase by Haeger in 1939. Based on the impressed mark, form and decoration, this example is believed to date approximately 1910–1930, most likely the 1920s.

The tall cylindrical form, substantial applied handle, defined pouring spout and bold blue banding give it especially strong primitive farmhouse presence. It would display beautifully on an open kitchen shelf, antique cupboard or country hutch, or as part of an early American pottery or yellowware collection.

CONDITION:
A long, fine hairline extends through the wall and is visible on both the interior and exterior, as shown clearly in the close-up photographs. The pitcher remains intact and displays beautifully, but because the hairline crack is structural, it is offered strictly for decorative use. It has not been tested for holding water and is not recommended for serving or storing liquids.

Additional age-related characteristics include overall glaze crazing, scattered surface wear, small marks, firing and glaze irregularities, and wear around the underside and base.
