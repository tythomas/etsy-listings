Description

Antique hand-pieced Eight Point Star quilt, circa 1900-1920, in double pink calico, blue-and-tan woven plaid, and soft solid pink on a white cotton ground. Twenty blocks, four stars to a block, eighty stars in all. Measures 88" x 72".

Made the way most turn-of-the-century farm quilts were, out of the cloth the household already had. The stars are set in bands rather than scattered -- a rose row at one end, a wide blue field through the middle, a pale pink row at the other. Hand pieced and hand quilted from edge to edge.

The date comes from the cloth, not a label. The printed pink is a "double pink," the small-figured cinnamon-pink calico common from the 1880s into the 1910s; the blue-and-tan check is a yarn-dyed woven plaid that reads the same on both faces, as work-shirt shirting did in those decades. Nothing is signed or dated, and no maker is known.

Each block sets four eight-pointed stars around a plain white cross, with a white square at the center and a small white diamond on point inside each star. The hand quilting is an even diagonal crosshatch carried right across the star blocks and the open white ground alike, close and consistent.

Care: given the thinning noted below, do not machine wash. Spot clean gently, or have it wet-cleaned by a textile conservator if it needs more.

CONDITION

• Small tan and brown age spots in the white cloth: one in the sashing between two rose blocks, another in a white patch near an edge. Both shown close up.
• One border shows a visible dark line that appears to be a quilting guide mark (pictured in grid photo and last full photo)
• Darkening of the fabric along the edging, from age and handling.
• General softening and light overall fading, consistent with the age.
• Light puckering where the white ground has relaxed around the quilting -- normal in a hand-quilted antique, not damage.
• The white pieces vary, some brighter and some creamier. Original -- the maker used more than one white cotton, not staining.
• No holes or tears.
• Hand soaked and air dried -- no odors.
