Description

An antique Fenton milk glass cake stand, 12 inches across, with a deeply crimped ruffle rim and a thumbprint-moulded pedestal foot.

Fenton pressed this form in opaque white milk glass through the 1960s and into the 1970s, and much of it went out unmarked through the company's Olde Virginia Glass line. That is why there is no logo underneath: Fenton did not begin marking milk glass with its oval script until 1970, and the Olde Virginia pieces were left unmarked deliberately. The attribution here rests on the mould rather than a signature, and the mould is distinctive — a completely plain plate paired with a conical foot pressed in rows of thumbprint ovals, matching documented examples of the same stand at the same size.

The rim is where all the decoration went. Instead of a pattern pressed into the plate, Fenton put it entirely in the edge, in deep evenly spaced crimps shaped by hand while the glass was still hot. The plate itself is left smooth and undecorated, which is exactly what makes it work under a cake — it frames what sits on it instead of competing with it. Below, the stem flares into a wide conical foot ringed with thumbprint ovals that pick up light from underneath.
