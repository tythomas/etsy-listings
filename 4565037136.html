Description

A beautifully hand-quilted American patchwork quilt dating approximately to the 1940s–early 1950s, worked in rich blue, soft cream and a wonderful blue-and-orange geometric print.

Measuring approximately 64" x 76", this quilt has a wonderfully graphic blue-and-white appearance from a distance, while closer inspection reveals considerably more detail. The larger patterned sections are individually pieced from geometric shapes, and the small touches of orange throughout the printed cotton add an unexpected bit of color.

The workmanship is especially noteworthy. The quilt is extensively hand quilted with approximately 7–8 stitches per inch in the area measured. Rather than relying only on simple straight quilting, the maker worked a variety of decorative designs throughout the quilt, including interlocking curved forms, diamonds and other geometric motifs. These designs are particularly beautiful when viewed from the solid light-colored reverse.

The quilt top is constructed from period cotton fabrics. The blue, orange and cream geometric print is consistent in appearance with printed feedsack and other utility cottons used during this era. Because the fabric was cut into small pieces throughout the quilt and no original sack markings, seams or selvages remain, feedsack origin cannot be conclusively documented.

The overall arrangement is a geometric checkerboard-style patchwork incorporating both solid and pieced sections. I have not assigned a traditional pattern name because I have not found a documented block pattern that matches the construction closely enough to identify it with confidence.

CONDITION
This quilt is in good vintage condition for its age and remains remarkably attractive and displayable.

There are no holes or tears in the quilt body and no heavy staining or soiling. A couple of areas have missing quilting stitches. The most noticeable age-related wear is along portions of the blue binding, where the fabric has become worn and frayed. A few very small age marks may be seen on the reverse.

Please review the close-up photographs carefully, as the binding wear has been intentionally photographed and is part of the quilt's condition.

The quilt has been gently hand soaked and sun dried.

The blue remains wonderfully strong, and the quilt's age-softened cotton, hand stitching and gentle puckering give it the texture and character that make old handmade quilts so special.

A striking piece of mid-century American needlework with enough graphic presence for today's interiors while retaining all of the workmanship and character of an authentically old hand-quilted textile.
