Description

Antique wooden loom shuttles from an industrial textile mill, each tipped in steel at both ends and fitted with its original spring bobbin clip. Sold individually or as a set of 2.

These are power-loom shuttles: the part that carried the weft thread across the warp, thrown through the shed and caught by the picker stick hundreds of times a minute. Every part of the shape is doing a job. The body is one piece of dense hardwood, hollowed into an open cavity to hold the bobbin. Both ends taper to a pointed steel tip so the shuttle could fly in either direction and take the impact. The deep reddish sheen on the wood is not a finish, it is decades of running through a loom.

Inside each cavity a spring-steel clip gripped the bobbin, held down by a steel tongue plate and a single slotted screw. Those tongue plates are stamped with a letter in a small rounded cartouche, legible as an S but too worn to attribute to a named mill supplier, so no maker is claimed here. The working face of each shuttle carries a checkered inlaid wear panel and a metal-grommeted eye where the thread ran out. Brass bosses and countersunk screws sit flush on the reverse.

All four came out of the same loom room and match in length, profile, wood and hardware, so a single reads as a complete object and a pair reads as a true matched pair — harder to assemble than four odd shuttles.
