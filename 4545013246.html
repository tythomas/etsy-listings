Description

Antique hand pieced and hand quilted donkey quilt, 61 x 90 inches, with fifteen donkeys in fifteen different colors marching across a cream ground. This is the Kansas City Star's 1931 "Giddap" block, one of the most charming figural patterns in American quilting.

In 1931 the Kansas City Star published a pieced donkey block called "Giddap, a Very Democratic Donkey," designed by the Kansas quilt historian Carrie A. Hall. It was given a running mate the same year, an elephant named "Ararat," and the pair arrived just in time for the 1932 Roosevelt and Hoover campaign. Midwestern quilters picked their side with a needle. Giddap won that vote handily, and far more donkey quilts survive today than elephant ones. This is one of them, made by hand from the solid cottons of the Depression years.

The evidence for the attribution is in the block itself. Each donkey stands in right profile, built from squares and rectangles, with small contrasting triangles doing the work of the ears, the eye, the hooves, the tail and the harness. That is exactly the construction the Star printed, and it matches the Giddap block held in the Spencer Museum of Art at the University of Kansas. The pattern is catalogued in Barbara Brackman's index of pieced quilt designs as number 226.77.

Fifteen donkeys are set three across and five down, and no two of them share a color. Gold with navy, teal blue with brown, mint green, burnt orange, chocolate brown with lavender, soft pink, tan, orchid with olive, moss green, cornflower blue, hot pink, and more besides. The blocks are separated by wide teal green sashing running one way and butter yellow sashing running the other, pinned together at every intersection by a small red square, and the whole quilt is bound in pale pink.

It is hand pieced and hand quilted throughout, in an overall grid of running stitch worked roughly an inch apart, with the quilting thread changed to suit the patch it crosses. The back is a plain cream cotton, and in several places a small hand quilted diamond and cross motif is worked in red thread, visible from the reverse. The batting is thin, so the quilt is light in the hand and drapes well over a rail or a sofa back.
