Description

A large Hawkeye picnic basket by the Burlington Basket Company, woven in deep forest green with a cream diamond pattern, 20 1/2 inches across, with its removable pie shelf and folding wood handles.

Burlington Basket Company of Burlington, Iowa named this line Hawkeye after their own state's nickname, and this is the big one — the full-size trunk basket rather than the little lunch size that turns up most often. The weave is rows of flat dyed splint in dark forest green, laced with cream cord, stepping through triangles and diamonds around all four sides — a real pattern worked into the weave rather than printed on it.

The lid is two hinged halves of wood-grained hardboard, split down the middle so one side opens while the other stays shut and keeps the flies out. Both wooden handles fold flat and outward on brass-plated pivots, which is what lets the closed lid work as a small table. Inside, a removable full-width pie shelf lifts out by a finger hole and sits on two metal brackets set into the basket walls — the shelf a pie or a cake rode on while the sandwiches travelled underneath. A second flat fiberboard panel sits below it, and one of the two carries a black heart painted on by hand at some point in its life; it is clearly somebody's addition rather than anything Burlington did.

About the attribution: no paper label survives. Burlington glued theirs inside the lid, where it is very commonly lost, and the underside of this lid is bare. The identification rests instead on the form and the pattern — the two-part hardboard lid, the folding wooden handles on brass pins, and this specific green diamond weave, all of which match documented labeled Hawkeye examples. 

Dating follows the same evidence. The wooden folding handles place it earlier than the metal swing-handle Hawkeyes, which points to roughly the 1950s to 1960s. Burlington built this form into the 1970s, so a later date cannot be ruled out — either way it is comfortably past forty years old.

Condition:
Good antique condition, structurally sound, with honest wear that is itemized here and photographed close up.

• Lid: a chip of the wood-grain surface is missing along the centre split, roughly an inch long — shown close up. Light scratches and a few small dark marks across the top.
• Base band: a short splinter of the bent wood band has lifted at one corner, and there is a hairline split in the band on another side. Both shown.
• Weave: one cream cord weaver has worked loose and stands proud near a corner post. Scattered light surface scuffing on the green splint, and a few pale abrasion marks where the finish has rubbed.
• Handles: both present and both pivot freely. The finish is worn with small dark marks and scattered nicks.
• Interior panels: the removable fiberboard pieces carry brown staining and small rust-colored spots.
• The hand-painted black heart on one panel is permanent and will come with the basket.
• Bottom: the pressed fiberboard base is sound, with scuffing and light soil.

Not flaws: the slight variation in the depth of green across the weave is how dyed splint ages and is not damage, and the cream cord reading warmer in some rows than others is the same thing.

Care: dust with a dry brush or vacuum on low suction. Do not wet the weave or the fiberboard panels — neither takes water well.

A basket this size earns its keep sitting still — the folding handles and flat lid mean it can also just be a side table that happens to open. And it still does the thing it was built for, if you want to carry lunch somewhere in it.
