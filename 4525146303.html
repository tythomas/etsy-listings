Hand quilted Orange Peel and Nine Patch quilt measuring 84 x 70 inches with colorful scrap fabrics, feedsack-era prints, Illinois provenance, a stitched 1957 date, and freshly hand washed and sun dried.

Hand quilted Orange Peel and Nine Patch quilt measuring approximately 84 x 70 inches.

This vintage quilt features a colorful assortment of cotton scrap fabrics set against a bright white background. The traditional Orange Peel and Nine Patch design creates a striking pattern that displays beautifully on a bed, quilt rack, or wall.

The quilt is hand pieced and hand quilted with cotton batting and a white cotton backing. Extensive quilting is visible throughout the quilt, and the piece has a soft, comfortable drape.

A family provenance card accompanies the quilt and identifies it as "Rocky Glen," noting that it was made by relatives of Eilene Young of Golden, Illinois. Research has not confirmed whether Rocky Glen refers to a family property, location, nickname, or quilt name, so that information is presented as part of the quilt's documented history.

The quilt also bears embroidered initials and a stitched date of 1957.

One of the most interesting aspects of this quilt is the variety of fabrics used in its construction. Several prints appear consistent with feedsack-era textiles from the 1930s and 1940s, while others appear slightly newer. The assortment suggests that older household fabrics may have been saved and incorporated into the quilt over time, a common practice in American scrap quilting traditions. The documented 1957 date creates an interesting connection between the quilt's known history and the earlier fabrics used throughout the piece.
